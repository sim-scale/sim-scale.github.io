Experiments

Experiment Setup

Three bimanual tasks in the real world, each evaluated on five sets of distinct objects with 20 rollouts per set. Policies are trained only in simulation and deployed zero-shot. Policy backends: GR00T N1.6 and DINOv3 + FMT.

SimScale Capabilities

Can one human demonstration train policies that transfer zero-shot to diverse real-world objects?

SimScale policies transfer zero-shot to diverse real-world objects with far less human effort.

With one human demonstration, SimScale outperforms Real-Demos (60 real-world demonstrations) by 30.0% and the strongest simulation baseline (120 human demonstrations) by 31.7% on average.

SimScale policies generalize across diverse object instances without overfitting to a subset of objects.

Baselines degrade sharply on objects absent from their training data. SimScale never sees any test object. Bars average three tasks and two policy backends.

On the same real objects, baselines trained with 120 human demonstrations often fail where SimScale succeeds.

SimScale Analysis: AssetEngine

All policies here use human source demonstrations, to isolate the effect of assets.

AssetEngine assets improve geometric generalization in sim-to-sim settings.

Policies trained on AssetEngine assets score highest on every held-out asset source, even on held-out RoboCasa assets.

AssetEngine assets improve zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

With the same demonstrations, AssetEngine assets give the strongest zero-shot transfer. RoboCasa has no chargers, and GPT-6-Astra assets were evaluated with DINOv3 + FMT only.

AssetEngine generates diverse and physically plausible assets.

Other methods give up either plausibility or diversity. In a user study, AssetEngine assets are rated the most plausible, with diversity comparable to Articraft.

SimScale Analysis: DemoEngine

All methods use the same 40 AssetEngine assets.

DemoEngine generates trajectories that match the quality of human-collected demonstrations.

One human demonstration matches re-collecting human demonstrations for every asset.

DemoEngine trajectories are more suitable for visuomotor policy training than datasets distilled from GPT-6-Astra programs.

GPT-6-Astra programs succeed in simulation but move unnaturally, and their policies almost entirely fail in the real world.

Conclusion

We presented SimScale, an agentic framework that scales up simulation training data for sim-to-real robot learning with only one human demonstration. Its AssetEngine authors category-level procedural generators that balance geometric diversity and physical plausibility, and its DemoEngine adapts the demonstration across the resulting geometries. Policies trained only on SimScale data transfer zero-shot to diverse real-world objects and outperform the strongest baseline by approximately 30% with far less human effort. These findings support category-level procedural generation as a practical approach to scaling up simulation data for sim-to-real robot learning.
